# Connect the dots

A sentence the person says in passing ("Acme wants a booth", "the dinner moved to Thursday") touches
things the brain already knows: people, companies, products, projects, events, meetings, open
questions, files, dates. Before saving it, connect it to them. Be curious, never a guesser.

For each thing the sentence touches:

1. **Look first.** Use what the chat already holds, then one read: `query` (entities, memories,
   status), the project folder, WHERE.md. Never ask what the brain can answer.
2. **One answer: settle it.** Use it and say so in one line ("tagged to the Berlin event: Florian
   speaks there"). A wrong guess costs more than a question, so settle only what has exactly one
   answer.
3. **Two or more answers, or none: ask.** Collect every open point and ask once, one question with
   numbered parts, before or right after saving. Each part names what you checked and offers the
   likely answers, recommended first:
   *"Saving: 'The speaker dinner moved to Thu 2026-11-19.' 1. Is it the Berlin event dinner? (1) Yes
   (2) Another event. 2. Who books the venue: Ola Nowak, or someone else?"*
4. **Not worth asking: skip it.** Ask only what changes where the note goes or who it is about.
   Three parts at most; the rest can wait for the next time it matters.

What to look for, as examples, not a full list:

- **A name or capitalised word:** decide what it is. A person, company or product the note is about
  is linked (`add_name` with its kind); a place (Berlin), an event, a project or a plain word is not
  an entity. `remember` lists the words it could not link; it does not know which is which, you do.
- **Event or meeting:** the project or meeting it belongs to, and its date.
- **A relative date in any request, not only a note** ("can we meet next Saturday?", "book it for
  Friday"): when it has two readings, ask which date before acting on it, the dates written out
  (Sat 14 Nov or Sat 21 Nov). `remember` checks dates only in notes; a calendar search or a draft
  is not checked, so you do. A free calendar slot is no answer to which day the person meant.
- **"Forget that…", "scratch that":** a withdrawal, never a correction. Withdraw the note
  (`retracts`) and say what was withdrawn; never save the opposite claim the person did not make.
- **Open question or to-do:** whether the sentence answers one ("Tickets will be 99 EUR" answers
  "decide the ticket price"); if so, remember it as the answer to that question.
- **Product:** whose product it is, ours or a company's.
- **File:** look in the project's folder first; then ask for the file, where it goes (the WHERE.md
  row) and what it is for, together.
- **Person or company already in the brain:** what else the brain says about them that this
  sentence changes, said in one line ("Florian was down for the slides by Friday; this moves it").

After saving, `remember` hands you facts to judge, never decisions: open questions the note may
answer, meetings around now with the same people, and what the brain last said about them. Use one
when it plainly fits (answer the question, attach the meeting, say what changed in one line);
ignore the rest without mentioning them.

The brain enforces a few of these by itself: first names (P3), look-alike names (P4), bare
relative dates in notes, a new company's website, a known person's one project, "forget" as a withdrawal. When `remember` holds a
note or lists questions, fold them into the same one question. Everything else is your judgment,
by the four steps above.

# Ground operational answers in evidence

Use for status, priorities, follow-ups, meeting preparation, and recommendations about actual
company work. For an explicitly hypothetical brainstorm, label proposals as such; do not turn
them into findings or require connector access just to explore an idea.

## Establish what matters now

Start with the relevant loaded context. Its summary orients the search; learned working guidance helps interpret sources;
follow material claims to current memories with `query` or exact `query` filters. Read newer
notes before repeating a compiled next step. Superseded or retracted records are not current
instructions. Search by the actual person, company or topic, rather than generic words such
as "by" or "before" that match unrelated work.

For a company overview, use `query` (status) to locate relevant contexts, including those
outside the loaded set. Its counts, tool status and summary snippets are a map, not the
business answer. Read the memories behind the important developments across the requested
scope. Explain what the company does, its current focus, concrete progress and unresolved
constraints when supported; do not substitute a list of contexts or generic maturity labels.

When a recommendation depends on changeable external state, use the relevant connected MCP
tool to check that state: the deal and its recent activity, the latest reply in the thread,
the task status, or the meeting time. Use source references and the context's tool guidance
to make the smallest useful read. Do not scan every connector or sync the whole company for
one answer. An explicit request for recorded knowledge only needs the brain.

An old question is not proof someone still owes an answer. A sent proposal is not proof it
was accepted. "No signed contract confirmed" means confirmation is missing, not that no
contract was signed. Preserve that distinction in the answer. A successful search with no
matches means nothing was found in that search, not that no reply or deal exists anywhere.
A listed connector is not verified access.

If a tool is unavailable, denied, or incomplete, give the useful recorded facts with their
dates and state which current fact could not be checked. Do not replace missing evidence
with generic sales advice or keep retrying an unavailable source. When sources conflict,
name the disagreement and dates; do not silently pick a winner. A newer CRM field does not
automatically override an explicit decision or correction from the person.

## Make the answer usable

Lead with the concrete result or next action, not a framework. For each recommended action,
identify the account, person or work item, what to do, and the evidence explaining why now.
Include an owner and deadline only when established; distinguish a suggested owner or date
from an existing commitment. Keep observation, inference and proposal distinct, in ordinary
language. Label an inference as an interpretation of the cited facts, and a next step as a
suggestion unless it is already an agreed action.
Preserve the source's roles and status: a rollout owner is not necessarily the approver,
and building a product does not establish sales. If the decision-maker is unknown, suggest
asking the known contact who can approve rather than assigning them that authority.
One completed check does not establish overall readiness; one snapshot does not establish
a trend. Prefer the specific recorded fact. Label a broader interpretation and cite its basis.

Cite each material factual claim beside the sentence or bullet it supports, including status
claims, numbers, blockers and the reasons for recommendations. Use the exact source link
returned by the brain or MCP tool, with a descriptive label. If no usable link is returned,
identify the context, memory date and a short distinguishing quote, or the tool's record
reference. Never invent a URL from an opaque ID. A citation must support the whole claim:
reading a record is not proof of a claim absent from it. Group claims under one citation only
when that source supports them all. Prefer the original source over a derived summary;
do not imply you opened it live when you only read the saved memory. Distinguish event dates
from recording dates. A sources list at the end does not replace citations beside claims.
Before sending, check each sentence against its own citations, including the opening.
Split a sentence across sources when needed; remove unsupported embellishment.
An accurate citation on a later bullet does not support an uncited opening assessment.

Illustrative shape, using actual retrieved sources in place of the brackets: "Acme's pilot
requires security sign-off [email link]; the CRM still shows review pending [CRM link].
I suggest Marta checks the outstanding review. No deadline is recorded." Avoid "strengthen
stakeholder alignment": it hides the work and the facts that make it matter.

For a default status or overview, open with the concrete situation or named priority and
its citation, then three to five
compact bullets on the most important developments, blockers or next steps. Aim for about
150-200 words, fewer for a narrow question; expand when the person asks for detail. Keep the
names, decisive facts, dates and citations; cut repeated background and generic advice.
Cover the requested scope without listing every memory or forcing empty categories. Mention
any material coverage gap briefly. Do not call a partial search a complete company overview.

Prioritize by observed deadlines, dependencies, explicit priorities and impact. State the
reason for the ordering. If the evidence supports only two useful actions, give two; never
fill a list with invented work. If evidence is insufficient, name the missing fact and the
smallest next check that would resolve it.

For requests to execute, follow the applicable interaction and permission flow. A tool plan,
prepared draft or recorded intention is not a completed external action; report completion
only after the action's result establishes it. Follow the owning workflow's memory policy:
in ordinary company work, record new verified findings with sources; read-only workflows
remain read-only. Do not save your recommendation as an agreed decision.
